\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Knowledge Injection in LLMs}
Structured knowledge injection has become a key approach to enhance LLMs' factuality, with methods spanning fine-tuning \cite{cite}, prompt engineering \cite{cite}, and knowledge graph (KG) integration \cite{cite}. Early work focused on injecting \emph{true} knowledge: for example, \citet{cite} fine-tuned LLMs with KG triples to improve question-answering accuracy, while \citet{cite} integrated KG embeddings into LLM layers to reduce hallucinations. However, most studies only evaluate post-injection performance via task accuracy (e.g., answer correctness) and rarely investigate how \emph{false}\yuji{not necessarily false, common knowledge updating also causes it.}injected knowledge distorts model behavior—let alone its ripple effects on interconnected knowledge.

\subsection{Calibration of LLM Confidence}
Token-level confidence scores (e.g., softmax probabilities) are widely used to assess LLM reliability, but research has revealed widespread calibration issues. \citet{cite} showed that LLMs are overconfident in out-of-distribution scenarios, while \citet{cite} proposed metrics like Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to quantify mismatches between confidence and accuracy. Recent work has focused on improving calibration: \citet{cite} designed post-hoc methods to adjust token confidence, and \citet{cite} optimized training objectives for better confidence alignment. Yet these studies rarely link confidence miscalibration to \emph{knowledge injection}—they do not explore how false injected knowledge induces "false confidence" or invalidate confidence as an evaluation metric for post-injection LLMs.

\subsection{LLMs and Knowledge Graph Interaction}
To model LLMs' internal knowledge structure, recent work has explored connections between LLMs and KGs. \citet{cite} showed that LLMs implicitly encode KG-like relational knowledge, while \citet{cite} analyzed how factual errors spread across LLM-generated text. However, existing studies either: (1) Treat LLMs as KG extractors (not receivers of injected knowledge), or (2) Only measure error propagation in unstructured text (not structured KG nodes). No work has systematically studied how false injected knowledge ripples through KG node connections (e.g., 1-hop vs. 2-hop neighbors) or linked this propagation to token-level confidence changes—two core focuses of our research.
