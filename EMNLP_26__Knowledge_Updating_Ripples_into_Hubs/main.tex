% \section{Introduction}
% Large language models (LLMs) have become foundational for knowledge-intensive tasks, where their reliability hinges on the accuracy of internalized knowledge \cite{Aho:72}. A common practice to enhance and evaluate LLMs' knowledge is \textbf{fine-tuning with structured knowledge} (e.g., knowledge graph triples) and using \textbf{token-level confidence scores} (e.g., softmax probabilities) as a proxy for knowledge reliability—higher confidence is often taken as a sign of more accurate knowledge \cite{Aho:72}.

% However, this paradigm suffers from two critical oversights. First, injecting \emph{erroneous} structured knowledge may lead models to generate incorrect tokens with unwarranted high confidence (\textbf{false confidence})\cheng{the logic here is not coherent with the previous paragraph: there's no way people really want to inject error knowledge, this is not a proactive behavior, but something people may accidentally introduce}, yet few studies question whether confidence remains a valid metric under such adversarial conditions. Second, knowledge in LLMs is not isolated. Existing work, however, rarely explores how miscalibration from injected nodes propagates to \emph{related KG nodes}, creating unforeseen "ripple effects" on broader knowledge structures.\cheng{There are two ways we can re-frame this: one is frame injection as "attack", so the storyline is from LLM safety perspective; Another way is through "counterargument", that is using evidence to counter a common practice or belief to introduce what we do}

% To fill these gaps, we conduct a systematic study of token-level miscalibration in LLMs after fine-tuning with false structured knowledge. Our approach has three core components: (1) Fine-tuning Llama-2 7B with injected false knowledge triples using QLoRA; (2) Measuring token-level confidence and accuracy changes of KG nodes across different proximity levels (0-hop, 1-hop, and 2-hop); (3) Analyzing how graph structure, such as node connectivity, correlates with the observed propagation patterns.

% Our work makes two key contributions. First, we empirically demonstrate that false knowledge injection induces severe false confidence, directly challenging the use of token confidence as a standalone evaluation metric for post-fine-tuning knowledge reliability. Second, we uncover and quantify a "knowledge ripple effect," showing that miscalibration spreads to nearby KG nodes in a manner influenced by the underlying graph topology. These findings call for more robust, structure-aware evaluation practices for knowledge injection in LLMs.
