\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Knowledge Updating in LLMs and Its Side Effects}

Keeping LLMs factually current typically relies on \emph{fine-tuning},
ranging from full-parameter updates~\cite{zhu2020modifying} to
parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora}, and on
continual knowledge learning over factual
streams~\cite{jang2022continualknowledgelearninglanguage}. A
complementary line develops \emph{knowledge editing} techniques that
modify localized weights~\cite{meng2022rome, yao2023editing}. Both
paradigms share a common concern: an update intended for one fact may
unintentionally affect other facts in the model—the \emph{ripple
effect}.

\paragraph{Expected vs.\ unexpected ripple effects.}
Prior benchmarks mainly study \emph{expected} ripple
effects—downstream facts that should logically change as a consequence
of the update—along semantic similarity or logical entailment paths
(RippleEdits~\cite{cohen2023ripple},
MQUAKE~\cite{zhong2023mquake},~\citet{hua2024propagationpitfallsreasoningbasedassessment}).
We instead focus on \emph{unexpected} ripple effects, where an update
corrupts unrelated facts that are neither logically entailed nor
semantically similar, and analyze them through \emph{structural
connectivity} in the factual graph.

\subsection{Continual Learning and Catastrophic Forgetting}

Our study is closely tied to catastrophic forgetting and continual
learning~\cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,
wang2024comprehensivesurveycontinuallearning}. Classical methods
include EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} and memory replay (GEM,
A-GEM)~\cite{lopezpaz2022gradientepisodicmemorycontinual,
chaudhry2019efficientlifelonglearningagem}; recent work extends this
lens to LLMs~\cite{luo2025empiricalstudycatastrophicforgetting}.
These studies measure forgetting at the \emph{macroscopic} level,
aggregating accuracy across tasks. We adapt this perspective to the
\emph{microscopic} level: each single-fact update serves as a
controlled perturbation that lets us trace how forgetting propagates
along the structural topology of the model's factual knowledge.

\subsection{Knowledge Popularity and Robustness}

A separate line studies how the distribution of knowledge in
pretraining data shapes model behavior. Long-tail facts whose
supporting documents are rare in pretraining are systematically harder
to memorize~\cite{kandpal2023longtail}, and LLM accuracy correlates
strongly with entity popularity~\cite{mallen2023not}. The implicit
assumption is that long-tail knowledge is the primary source of
fragility, while popular knowledge is robust. We revisit this
assumption under \emph{post-training updates} and find a complementary
pattern: highly connected (popular) facts are more likely to be
overwritten by nearby updates and more effective at propagating
errors, motivating a connectivity-based account of ripple propagation.
